# Market study → target design for Ordinary Lives

> Standing mandate: study the market's best 2D games (highlights **and** flaws), design toward a bar that is **fun, worth paying for, worth opening every day, and leaves a person with something for their own life** — then build and iterate past it.

## 1. What the best 2D games do right — and where each falls short

| Game | Why people love / pay / return | Its flaw (what I must NOT copy) |
|---|---|---|
| **Balatro** | Irresistible juice; "one more run"; push-your-luck brain-tickle; cheap; generous free updates | Almost no narrative or stakes beyond a score; theme is thin |
| **Hades** | Story advances *through* failure (every death feeds the plot); characters you grow to love; tight, expressive combat | Gated behind twitch skill; run-to-run room-clearing gets repetitive |
| **Stardew Valley** | Gentle daily rhythm (days, seasons); dozens of interlocking goals; cozy; enormous content | Slides into chore/optimization; time-pressure guilt; dated UI |
| **Hollow Knight** | Atmosphere; exploration that *rewards* curiosity; mastery; secrets | Punishing difficulty; backtracking; vague objectives |
| **Return of the Obra Dinn** | Pure deduction — you cross-reference clues and the game confirms *your* reasoning | One-and-done (no reason to replay); dry; niche |
| **Papers Please** | The mechanic *is* the fiction; mounting stakes; real moral weight; many endings | Repetitive daily loop; trial-and-error rule-learning; bleak |
| **Vampire Survivors** | Power fantasy; near-zero input; visual spectacle; cheap | Shallow and passive |
| **Slay the Spire** | Build variety; every decision matters; huge replayability | Steep mastery; RNG can feel unfair |
| **Animal Crossing** | A gentle reason to open it *every day*; no fail state; collection | Time-gated by the real clock; can feel empty of purpose |
| **Celeste** | Flow and mastery; an **assist mode that respects you**; a story about anxiety | Hard; short |
| **Undertale** | Choices visibly matter; charm; music that carries feeling | Occasional bullet-hell frustration |
| **Firewatch / Edith Finch** | Emotional payoff; craft | Almost no interactivity; single playthrough |

## 2. Synthesized principles (steal) and anti-patterns (avoid)

**Steal**
- **Progress = story** (Hades): never waste a player's effort on a dead-end; every action reveals more of the people.
- **The player's own reasoning, confirmed** (Obra Dinn): the joy is "I worked it out," validated by the game — not "I picked the right option."
- **The verb is the fiction** (Papers Please): the core action is the job, not a menu in front of it.
- **Juice on every action** (Balatro): sound + motion + light make a correct move *feel* correct.
- **A gentle daily rhythm with no fail state** (Stardew / Animal Crossing): a reason to return that never punishes absence.
- **Escalating power/knowledge fantasy** (VS / Outer Wilds): learning one thing recontextualizes the world.
- **Respect** (Celeste assist mode): difficulty is offered, never forced; no punishment for uncertainty.

**Avoid**
- One-and-done with no reason to return (Obra Dinn, Firewatch).
- Twitch-skill gates and chore/optimization guilt (Hades' ceiling, Stardew's slippage).
- Passive shallowness (Vampire Survivors) and dry, juiceless deduction.
- **Manipulative retention**: no FOMO, no countdown timers, no paid energy, no shame for missing a day (per ethical "hot-streak" design). The daily pull must be an invitation, not a leash.

## 3. Target design for Ordinary Lives

**One line:** a *deduction game about ordinary dignity*, where you connect the records a station tried to erase — and where the station keeps a small light on for you every day.

Hitting the four bars:
- **Fun core** — the **LINK** mechanic: records contain tangible tokens; you connect the true pair; wrong links are a soft "not quite," never punished; the correct link unlocks the reveal with juice. Different verbs per act, space-native (align a timeline, triangulate a signal across light-delay, rebuild a route). → DESIGN-2D.md Phases 2–3.
- **Worth paying for** — a complete, moving 4×6 narrative + six case files + genuine replay (four lives, branching outcomes, a different ending each) + the daily ritual below. A finished game you *remember*, priced like a good book.
- **Worth opening every day** — **"Today at Homeward / 今日 · 归港"**: one quiet moment from the station's fifty years, the same all day and new each dawn, gathered into a **fragment box**. No timer, no penalty for missing days; the box fills at your own pace.
- **Leaves something for your life** — each daily moment and each ending is a small, true thing about ordinary people: the gap between a system's record and a lived life, small dignities, what we leave behind. Values-driven, not preachy (Jerrett et al.: meaning through the interactivity itself).

**Anti-goals (protect the soul):** no ads, no accounts, no paid energy, no countdown, no FOMO. Daily return is an invitation.

## 4. Build order (each phase: build → full test green → commit → push)

1. ✅ **Phase 1** — deduction framed as evidence→meaning; "insight" chord; reverb/air/motif; act-aware ambient light.
2. **Phase 1.5 (this pass)** — **Today at Homeward**: the daily moment + fragment box (daily return + collection + life-inspiration, done tastefully).
3. **Phase 2** — the real **LINK** mechanic (tangible tokens; connect the true pair; confirmed reveal).
4. **Phase 3** — varied per-act verbs + space mechanics (timeline, light-delay triangulation, route rebuild).
5. **Phase 4** — living scenes (light breath, idle character, environmental motion), expressive portraits.
6. **Phase 5** — a full game-feel / juice pass on every interaction.
